\section{Why It Works: the Masked S-box Head Is a Carrier}
\label{sec:carrier}

\subsection{Top-1 accuracy is the wrong measure}
In every working model the $C$ heads are barely above chance (residual networks 1.40--1.53\%, GPAM
0.93--1.00\%, chance 0.39\%), yet replacing them by a uniform distribution makes all 16 key bytes flat,
while replacing them by the true labels lifts every working model to a median $z$ of +15.7 to +15.9 and
rank 0 on every byte after 10 traces (one ordering). The key information reaches the decoder
only through $C$; $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\pi$ decide how much of it survives the marginalisation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decode}. Even the mean log-likelihood gain of $C$ on its own label is not a reliable
proxy: the overfitted 137.4M checkpoint has a negative gain ($-0.48$ nats) and still recovers the
key. \todo{a key-discrimination measure, e.g.\ per-trace log-likelihood ratio of the true key after
mask marginalisation, plotted against $z$ for all models}

\subsection{A shared bottleneck extinguishes the carrier}
Variant A (shared 512-d bottleneck) and variant B (unshared heads) have the same trunk and nearly the
same size (15.09M vs.\ 15.65M). At
50k profiling traces B recovers the key and A recovers no byte (median $z=-0.29$). Oracle swaps
locate the failure: with A's own $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\pi$ and the true $C$, the attack reaches
$z=+15.74$; with true $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\pi$ and A's own $C$, it stays at $+0.04$. The trunk and
the high-SNR heads work; the low-SNR $C$ heads lose the shared subspace.

learned, $C$ at chance); GPAM at LR $10^{-3}$ shows a shared representation can carry $C$ when
trained long enough -- phrase as an empirical finding, not a principle}
