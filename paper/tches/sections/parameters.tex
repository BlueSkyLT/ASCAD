\section{Where the Parameters Go}
\label{sec:params}

\subsection{MultiResNetSCA-1}
The network of~\cite{MS23} is a one-dimensional residual network~\cite{ResNet} with a stem
convolution and nine stacks of residual blocks (kernel size 11, stride 2 from the second stack,
channels doubling from 16 to a cap of 256). For a 15,000-sample trace the last stack outputs
$256\times 59$ features; an average pooling of width 4 and a flatten give a 3,584-dimensional
vector $h$. Each of the 34 targets has its own head
$\mathrm{Linear}(3584,1024)\to\mathrm{ReLU}\to\mathrm{BN}\to\mathrm{Linear}(1024,K)$ with
$K\in\{256,16\}$.

\label{tab:params}
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}

\midrule
Convolutional trunk & 7,525,472 & 7,525,472 & 7,525,472 \\

\smallskip
{\footnotesize For variant A, ``Heads'' includes the shared $3584\to512$ bottleneck (1,836,544
parameters); ``first dense layers'' counts the 34 per-head $512\to256$ layers only.}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:params} shows that 94.5\% of the parameters are in the heads and 90.8\% in their 34
first dense layers alone, each a $3584\times1024$ matrix. The trunk, which is the only part that
sees the time axis, is 5.5\% of the model. Because $h$ keeps 14 coarse time positions, each head can
also index \emph{where} a feature occurs, which is useful for the permutation heads but gives every
head a large, position-indexed capacity to fit noise.

\subsection{CompactMultiResNet}
We keep the trunk unchanged and replace every head by
$\mathrm{Linear}(3584,64)\to\mathrm{ReLU}\to\mathrm{BN}\to\mathrm{Linear}(64,K)$ (variant B,
15.65M parameters). Variant A instead inserts one \emph{shared} 512-dimensional bottleneck between
$h$ and 256-wide heads (15.09M). Both are 8.8--9.1$\times$ smaller than MultiResNetSCA-1 and within 4\% of each other in size; they
differ mainly in whether the 34 targets share a dense representation. Section~\ref{sec:carrier}
shows that this difference decides whether the attack works.
