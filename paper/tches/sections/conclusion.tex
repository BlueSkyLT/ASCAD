\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The reference worst-case attack on ASCADv2 spends almost all of its parameters on what its heads
read, not on what its trunk extracts. Removing that excess keeps the attack at the published trace
count, makes it robust to the choice of checkpoint, and exposes the mechanism: the masked S-box heads
carry the key at near-chance accuracy, and sharing a dense representation suppresses them. The label
ladder shows which mask-head losses a decoder loss can replace; it does not remove label information
from profiling, and learning the masks from $S$ alone is untested.
